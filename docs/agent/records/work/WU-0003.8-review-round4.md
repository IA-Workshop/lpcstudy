**R26 (low; plan level, for the planner; the a7 question asked of this read). Bitwise `fitted_inverse_dim` is the right criterion on the run's own stack, and the wrong one across stacks. The tool cannot tell the two cases apart.**
- **What I find.**
  - The inputs of the fit are reproduced bitwise first: `median_seed_by_n_lib` is a selection and a correctly rounded √, so it is stack-independent.
  - With equal inputs, `curve_fit` (TRF, 2-point Jacobian) on the same numpy, scipy, BLAS and CPU is deterministic. Bitwise is therefore achievable, and it is the strongest test that the tool's copy equals `_fit_seed_law`. Q1 §Q1.3 confirms the leaves are normally bitwise in T1 and T3.
  - On another stack, Q1 shows that the FD Jacobian amplifies last-bit differences in exp and log by about 10⁷:
    - up to 2e-7·s with no change of the stopping iteration;
    - up to 7.4e-6·s measured, and 4e-5·s a priori for a pair of correct fits (Q1 §Q1.3), when the stop flips.
  - A bitwise check there gives MISMATCH (exit 1, a defect, the series dropped) on correct code.
  - That error is in the strict direction: nothing reads as passing. But it would send a correct cell to a DEF investigation.
- **ψ hardly depends on this leaf.**
  - The weights come from the tight refit θ*, which is already required to lie within 4e-5·s of the production fit.
  - The production fit enters ψ only through R̂, at a relative size of about 1e-4 at most.
- **Recommendation (no change now).**
  - Keep the bitwise criterion when the analysis stack equals the run's `provenance`. `results.json` records numpy and scipy (`provenance.PACKAGES`) and the CPU model and ISA.
  - When the stack differs, print it, and compare against the Q1 a-priori pair bound 4e-5·s, the same constant as `REFIT_TOLERANCE`, which is derived, not tuned:
    - within the bound: "not computable: bitwise reproduction needs the run's stack" (exit 2), never exit 0;
    - beyond the bound: MISMATCH.
  - Until the planner rules, the developer could at least print the stack comparison beside the a7 line. The a7 one-ulp test stays as the same-stack case.
