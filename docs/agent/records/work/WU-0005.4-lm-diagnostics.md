### M1. Residual: correct, with an addition

- **Why c and not a transform.** c = ‖X_t − G′(û)‖² is the objective that LM actually minimizes and the quantity it compares. The RMS √(c/D) and c/ρ² are monotone transforms of it. Under a positive scaling, order-statistic quantiles commute exactly, and the median and linear quantiles commute up to one rounding. c/ρ² can therefore be derived in the report from c and the cell's ρ, and no results key is needed. Reporting c keeps the summary independent of a ρ convention.
- **Reading guide verified.** The noise is ρz in normalized units (`ensemble.py:353`, so the noise standard deviation is ρ exactly). If the model is exact and the estimate is converged in the correct basin, then to first order in ρ, c/ρ² ~ χ²_{D−m} = χ²₄, with median 3.357 and p95 9.488. Probe values (n = 64, clean start, ρ ∈ {0.01, 0.05}, τ ∈ {2, 5}):
  - median c/ρ² from 3.08 to 3.26;
  - Kolmogorov–Smirnov p from 0.44 to 0.60 against χ²₄.
- **Addition (recommended, no new key).** The RESULTS.md row prints the median and the p95 of c/ρ² beside c, with the header "first-order reference for a converged estimate in the correct basin: χ²₄ median 3.36, p95 9.49". It is a reading guide, not a criterion. All noise levels are positive (`config.py:118`), so c/ρ² is always defined.
- If an RMS is ever printed, it must be √(c/(D−m))/ρ, which is about 1. The plan's √(c/D) is not the right normalization: E c = (D−m)ρ², not Dρ².

### M2. Non-increase and L2

- **L1(a): correct.** The carry takes c_cand only when c_cand < c_u, so c_k ≤ c_{k−1} holds by construction, with c_k < c_{k−1} exactly at the accepted iterations. L1(a) tests the bookkeeping, as the plan says.
- **Null steps: correct, but the reading must be documented.** "Accepted" means accepted by the algorithm. Round-off accepts are real and frequent:
  - At the true start with no noise, there are 0 to 2 accepts per estimate (τ = 2 and 5), all at round-off level.
  - With noise (ρ = 0.01 and 0.05), 200 of 376 and 248 of 470 accepts had a relative decrease below 10⁻⁸. The median number of accepts after the cost converged (to a relative 10⁻¹⁰) was 3 per estimate.
  - In the 256-state probes, the median `accepted` was 7 to 10, while the median number of *material* accepts (relative decrease > 10⁻⁶) was 2 to 5.

  The report must therefore say that `median_iterations` counts accepted steps, including the round-off accepts after convergence (so it is an upper bound on the productive steps).
- **L2 tolerance: wrong as stated.** The plan's |c_k − J(u_k)| ≤ 10⁻¹²·J(u_k) is not a round-off bound.
  - Let e be the difference between the two residual vectors (in-loop and eager compile). Then |c − J| = |‖r‖² − ‖r+e‖²| ≤ ‖e‖(2√J + ‖e‖), so the relative error is about 2‖e‖/√J.
  - In L2 there is no noise, and J falls quadratically to the round-off floor (probe: from about 10⁻³¹ to 10⁻²⁸). As J falls, a single ulp of difference in e (about 10⁻¹⁶) gives a relative error of order 1.
  - The plan's criterion passes only when the two compiles happen to agree bitwise. They did on this host (difference exactly 0 in every probe), but that is not guaranteed.

  **Documented correction (made before any code; it replaces an invalid bound and does not relax a valid one).** For each accepted k:

  |c_k − J(u_k)| ≤ η·(2√J(u_k) + η), with η = 10⁻¹²·max(1, ‖X‖_∞)

  - η is a bound, in normalized observation units, on the round-off difference between two XLA-CPU compilations of the same discrete map over a window of at most 1 Lyapunov time.
  - Random-walk estimate: √120 RK4 substeps × 4 ulp × |s|ε (about 9·10⁻¹⁵) × tangent growth of at most 30, divided by σ_x, which gives about 1.5·10⁻¹². Observed: below 10⁻¹⁵. So η is about 10³ above the observed difference.
  - The monotonicity of J along the accepted iterates, J(u_k) ≤ J(u_{k−1}) + b_k + b_{k−1}, where b is the bound above, follows from L1(a) and this bound. Assert it in that form, not as (1 + 10⁻¹²).
- **δ for L2.**
  - ‖δ‖ = 0.01 in the test's u coordinates (identity whitening, so physical units), with the direction drawn from a literal `default_rng`.
  - States on the attractor after a burn-in of at least 3000 steps, τ ≤ 5, `refine_steps = 15` set explicitly (the dataclass default is 6).
  - Evidence: at ‖δ‖ ∈ {10⁻³, 10⁻², 10⁻¹} all 64 of 64 states converged, with a final c ≤ 1.7·10⁻²⁸, and every state had at least 4 accepts. The basin edge appears at ‖δ‖ = 0.5 (τ = 5: one state with c = 5·10⁻⁵), and at ‖δ‖ = 1 there was a wrong basin (c = 13.7). So 0.01 is 10 to 50 times inside the edge.
  - Keep `accepted ≥ 1`. The final criterion is as in M3.

### M3. 10⁻¹²·D: correct but too loose, tightened

- **What L1(b) actually tests.** Monotonicity gives final ≤ c(u0) = ‖X_ref − G′(u_true)‖² exactly. L1(b) is therefore a test of the agreement between `simulate_trajectory` and `delay_map_forward`, plus the bookkeeping.
- **Observed.** The maximum over 64 attractor states was 2.2·10⁻³¹ at τ = 2 and 5.2·10⁻³¹ at τ = 5.
- **Tightened criterion (L1(b) and the L2 final residual).** final residual ≤ D·η² = 7·(10⁻¹²·max(1, ‖X‖_∞))², which is about 4·10⁻²³ for normalized X.
  - Margin: at least 10⁷ above the observed values.
  - It detects what 7·10⁻¹² misses. An accidental float32 path gives per-component errors of about 10⁻⁷ times the amplification, so c ≈ 10⁻¹³ to 10⁻¹², which sits at or below the plan's bound but about 10 orders above the new one (CLAUDE.md: float64 throughout).
- **Conditions the test must fix**, because the bound depends on them:
  - obs_mean and obs_scale set to the mean and σ_x of x on the test trajectory, so that X = O(1); or else keep the ‖X‖_∞ factor;
  - the target built with the **same `step` object** through `simulate_trajectory` (the same discrete map, LPC.NV.1). A different dt would add discretization error, not round-off;
  - τ ≤ 5;
  - at least 8 states in the batch, so that both branches occur. At the truth, accepts and rejects are both observed (0 to 2 accepts per state).

### M4. `fraction_no_decrease` and convergence

- **What `accepted = 0` means.** The largest damping actually used is 10⁻³·4¹⁴ ≈ 2.7·10⁵. The plan's 4¹⁵ ≈ 10⁶ is the value after the last rejection, which is never used. As the damping grows, the step tends to a small scaled gradient step, which decreases a smooth cost unless:
  - (a) the cost at u0 is non-finite (S3);
  - (b) the gradient is corrupted by zeroed Jacobian entries (S4);
  - (c) u0 is already stationary to round-off (measure zero for noisy data).

  So `fraction_no_decrease` is a **failure indicator**. It was 0 of 1024 in the noisy probes. The report should place it next to the S3 and S4 counts.
- **What `median_iterations` means.** It counts accepts, including round-off accepts (M2). It is not the number of iterations to convergence.
- **Neither key detects non-convergence at the 15-iteration budget.** Probe 3 and probe 4 used 256 states, a 30-iteration reference on common inputs, and counted an estimate as "unconverged at 15" when ‖u₁₅ − u₃₀‖ > 0.1·‖u₃₀ − u_true‖:

  | coordinates | start error | ρ | unconverged at 15 | c₃₀/ρ² > χ²₄(0.999) |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | identity | 0.05 | 0.02, 0.05 | 5 and 8 of 256 | 3 of 256 |
  | identity | 0.5 | 0.02, 0.05 | 8 and 10 of 256 | 12 to 13 of 256 |
  | whitened (as production) | 0.02 | 0.01, 0.05 | 8 and 9 of 256 | 4 to 6 of 256 |
  | whitened | 0.2 | 0.01, 0.05 | 34 and 32 of 256 | 41 to 45 of 256 |

### M5. Quantiles with inf: wrong with linear interpolation

Measured with numpy 2.5.3. The linear method returns NaN, with a RuntimeWarning (`invalid value encountered in subtract`), in these cases:
- the bracketing pair is (finite, inf) and the fractional index t is 0. For example, values 0..19 plus one inf (n = 21): q·(n−1) = 19 exactly, the true 95% order statistic is 19.0, and the linear method gives NaN;
- the bracketing pair is (inf, inf).

So "non-finite quantile → None" would report None for a finite quantile.

**Correction:** p95 uses `np.quantile(r, 0.95, method="inverted_cdf")`, the order statistic x_(⌈qn⌉) (Hyndman–Fan type 1). It is never NaN on NaN-free input, it is inf exactly when x_(⌈qn⌉) is inf, and inf maps to None alongside `nonfinite_residuals`. The residual is never NaN, because `cost` maps every non-finite value to inf. `np.median` may stay: (a + b)/2 with a ≤ b gives inf without NaN, and inf only when the upper middle order statistic is inf.

**L5 references** (closed form, from the order statistics), expected values for p95 and the median:

| values | p95 | median |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0..19 and one inf (n = 21) | 19.0 | 10.0 |
| 0..17 and two inf (n = 20) | None | 9.5 |
| 0..18 and one inf (n = 20) | 18.0 | 9.5 |
| a single inf | None | None |
| an empty array | n_estimates 0, all values None | |

Avoid hand cases where qn is within 10⁻⁹ of an integer, other than the documented n = 20 cases.

### M6. Counter units and names: correct

- **Unit.** A per-estimate boolean is the right unit for "denominator = estimates" (DEC-0017 Q4 precedent). `lm_nonfinite_cost` and `lm_nonfinite_jacobian` describe it correctly.
- **`isinf` is exact.** `cost` maps exactly the non-finite raw values to +inf, and a sum of squares is never −inf, so `isinf` on the mapped value is exact. It also counts overflow to +inf of a finite sum, which is a non-finite value under NVVUQ.11.
- **Scope of the per-iteration test.** It must test c0 and every c_cand, not only the carried c.
- **`refine_steps = 0`** is a supported value ("0 disables refinement") that has no defined diagnostics. Define: residual = cost(u0), accepted = 0, `nonfinite_cost` = isinf(cost(u0)), `nonfinite_jacobian` = False. It is not exercised by the pipeline.

### L3(ii): test construction must change

The forward-mode tangent of `jnp.full_like(s, nan)` and of `s + nan` is finite. Measured through `delay_map_forward`:
- with either stub, 0 of 21 Jacobian entries are non-finite, so `nonfinite_jacobian` would be False and L3(ii) would fail for a reason in the test, not in the code;
- with `lambda s: s * jnp.nan`, 18 of 21 entries are non-finite (row 0 is the observation of the start state and stays finite).

With that stub, A^T(X − G) contains 0·NaN, so the candidate is NaN, its cost is inf, it is rejected, and `accepted` = 0. The advanced state is NaN, which gives `invariant_mean_replacements` = n, and ν is NaN, so it is replaced. **Use `s * jnp.nan`.** L3(i) is correct as stated: the Jacobian does not depend on the target, so `nonfinite_jacobian` is False.

### L1(c), L1(d), L4: feasibility

- **Equality checks.** L1(c)/(d) and L4 rest on the same assumption: two XLA-CPU programs that differ only in their outputs do identical float arithmetic. At the true start, accept decisions are decided by ulps, so any re-fusion shows up there first.
  - Probe 2: the pre-change production `StateEstimator` and a separately compiled `jit(vmap)` trace variant agree **bitwise** on 64 noisy estimates on this host.
  - Requirement: build the trace variant from the same `body` closure under `jit(vmap(·))`, as production, and not unbatched. Batching changes the linear-solve kernel.
  - A mismatch is a finding (the L4/V1 risk), not a tolerance.
- **L4 additions** (tightening):
  - at least 4 rows started exactly at u_true, where accept decisions are ties;
  - one call on the `s * jnp.nan` estimator, so that the rebound `isfinite(a_mat)` mask path is covered, comparing the `estimate_counted` outputs (finite after replacement) plus both flags.

### M7. Reproducibility and the proposal for WU-0003.4

- **Same host, `LPC_THREADS` and BLAS kernel:** bitwise (V1).
- **Across hosts**, the cause is DEF-0024 (W and U differ by ulps), which may change the trained network and so u0.
  - **Converged, same basin.** c_min = min_u ‖X − fwd(mean + U u)‖² does not depend on U, mean or u0, because these only reparametrize u. It changes only through round-off in G: δc ≈ 2‖r‖‖δG‖, a relative change of at most about 2η/(ρ√χ²) ≈ 10⁻⁹. Post-convergence tie flips change `accepted` by ±1 but not c materially.
  - **Unconverged at budget, or wrong basin** (a few % to more than 10%, M4): if u0 changes macroscopically, c and `accepted` change by O(1).
- **Proposed tolerances**, to be confirmed by WU-0003.4's own CPU-variant measurement before confirmatory use (NVVUQ.12):
  - (i) **Network weights bitwise equal** across the two runs: per-estimate residual relative 10⁻⁸ outside the estimates flagged by D1; summaries relative 10⁻⁸; `fraction_no_decrease` exact; `median_iterations` within ±1.
  - (ii) **Weights differ** (GPU against CPU, or a different BLAS kernel): distributional. Let q be the run's median or p95.
    - The run's q must lie between the reference's order statistics at ranks nq ∓ 2.58√(nq(1−q)): a distribution-free 99% band, assuming independent estimates. It is conservative under the positive correlation of the oracle draws of one state; to state it precisely, use the effective n per state block.
    - |Δ `fraction_no_decrease`| ≤ 3√(2p̄(1−p̄)/n) + 1/n.
    - |Δ `median_iterations`| ≤ 1.
