## 0. Answer

**An influence series exists and is usable.** For each system and noise level ρ, over the kept test-sweep states in time order:

ψ_i = [ Σ_j A_j · s_ij / ĝ_j − R̂ · (v_i − μ̂) ] / μ̂,

where:
- s_ij = ½ − 1{e²_ij < m̂_j} − ½·1{e²_ij = m̂_j} is the sign type of the median. Here e²_ij = `h1a/rho=<g>/seed_sq`[i, j] and m̂_j is the column median.
- ĝ_j is the density of log e²_.j at log m̂_j: Siddiqui–Bloch–Gastwirth with the Hall–Sheather bandwidth.
- A_j = ½·[H⁻¹Jᵀ]_{2/d, j}, with H the exact Hessian of the least-squares fit at its minimiser (implicit-function theorem; §2.3).
- v_i = `h1a/inverse_dim`[i], μ̂ = mean(v), and R̂ = (1/d_fit)/μ̂, the H1a `ratio` leaf.

The first term alone (without the division by μ̂) is the influence series of `fitted_inverse_dim`.

**Verification** (§3).
- The analytic influence function matches a numerical Gateaux derivative to **2.2e-9 of max|ψ|**: 12 pilot cells × 60 contamination points, central difference h = 1e-5, tolerance 1e-8. Observed order 2.00 under step refinement.
- On finite samples, the series reproduces the Monte Carlo SD of the statistic to within 1–6% (iid) and 0.5–1% (MBB-5 form at φ = 0.3).
- The density estimate moves acf(1) by an SD of 0.003–0.009. That is 1/6 to 1/10 of acf(1)'s own sampling SD.

**Case value: none can be borrowed.** H1a has no WU-0006 coverage pair, so the E2 interpolation cannot be applied. The influence lag-1 at latent φ = 0.3 is not a constant for H1a: it ranges from 0.02 to 0.19 with the cross-size correlation of the seed errors (§4). Read H1a under "coverage not tabulated", with the φ = 0 reading only, plus the M4.2 block-factor reading β̂₅ computed from ψ. **Do not borrow C3's 0.19.** That would be looser.

## 1. The statistic (code at 0e9b892)

For each ρ, on the states `keep` (every `seed_sq` over all ρ and `inverse_dim` finite; time order kept):
- m_j = median over states of e²_ij, for j = 1..5 (n_lib = 1000, 3162, 10⁴, 31623, 10⁵);
- y_j = log m_j;
- θ = (a, b, c) = (log c², 2/d, log f²) minimises F(θ) = ½ Σ_j (g(x_j; θ) − y_j)², where x_j = log n_j and g = log(e^{a−bx} + e^c). The fit is `curve_fit` with TRF, the scipy default tolerances and a 2-point Jacobian, with b bounded to [0.01, 10];
- 1/d_fit = b/2, μ̂ = mean(v_i), and T = (b/2)/μ̂ (the `ratio`).

The moving-block bootstrap (block 5) resamples the kept states jointly for every column and for v.

## 2. Derivation (each step: status, hypothesis)

### 2.1 Median of a column

The influence function of the median at a state is IF_j(e²) = sign(e² − m_j)/(2 f_j(m_j)).

- **Status: correct**, if f_j is continuous and positive at m_j (**H-a**).
- The squared seed errors are continuous (squared distances of whitened states), so the median has no atom. The mid-rank form s_ij gives 0 to an observation equal to the sample median (odd n), as in M4.1.

The fit uses log m_j, and the log is monotone. So the median of log e² equals log m_j exactly, and

IF(log m_j) = s_j/g_j, with g_j = m_j f_j(m_j) the density of log e²_.j at log m_j (with s = ±½, s/g = sign/(2g)).

- **Status: correct.**
- Estimating g on the log scale is better conditioned: the log removes most of the right skew.

### 2.2 Validity of the linearization under serial dependence

The step is T̂ − T = mean_i ψ(X_i) + o_p(n^{−1/2}). It relies on the Bahadur representation of the sample median under dependence.

- **Status: needs a hypothesis (H-b):** the state sequence is strongly mixing, with summable mixing coefficients (Sen 1972; Wu 2005, as standard results; not re-verified, so `researcher` should confirm the citations before they enter a study document).
- **Checked numerically** (§3.2): corr(T̂ − T₀, L) = 0.91–0.97 and sd(T̂)/sd(L) = 0.98–1.05. Here L is the exact first-order term, with population medians and densities.

### 2.3 The fit map y ↦ b (implicit-function theorem)

**Stationarity.** Jᵀr = 0, where r = g(θ) − y and J = ∂g/∂θ. Write p = e^{a−bx}/(e^{a−bx} + e^c). Then:
- ∂g/∂(a, b, c) = (p, −xp, 1 − p);
- ∇²_θ g = p(1 − p)·v vᵀ, with v = (1, −x, −1).

**Differentiating the stationarity condition in y:** H dθ = Jᵀ dy, so

dθ/dy = H⁻¹Jᵀ, with H = JᵀJ + Σ_j r_j p_j(1 − p_j) v_j v_jᵀ.

- **Status: correct**, given:
  - **H-c:** θ̂ is interior. b is not at 0.01 or 10, and c is not at −60. The production flag is `n_exponent_at_bound.point`.
  - **H-d:** H is positive definite.
- On the 12 pilot cells, cond(H) = 7.4e3 to 1.34e4 and λ_min(H) = 4.2e-3 to 2.4e-2. No cell is at a bound (`ma1_gateaux.log`).

**The residual-curvature term is required.** The pilot residuals reach 0.11 in log. The Gauss–Newton form (JᵀJ)⁻¹Jᵀ errs by up to **24%** of max|A| (P1 Rössler ρ = 0.02; `ma1_ift.log`).

**Exact identity: Σ_j A_j = 0.**
- Proof: g(x; a + t, b, c + t) = g(x; a, b, c) + t. So a common shift of y is absorbed by (a, c), and b is invariant. Hence ∂b/∂y · 1 = 0.
- Observed: ≤ 1.1e-14 of max|A| (`ma1_sumzero.log`).
- **Consequence:** a state whose errors lie above (or below) the median at every library size contributes ½ Σ_j A_j/g_j to ψ. That is zero when the g_j are equal. H1a's 1/d responds to a state's *pattern across library sizes*, not to its overall difficulty. So serial dependence in the common difficulty of the states is largely invisible to H1a, both to its influence series and to its interval. This drives §4.

### 2.4 Composition and the ratio

**Composition.** The quantile functional is Hadamard differentiable at F when H-a holds (van der Vaart 1998, §21.2). The fit map is C¹ near θ̂ (H-c, H-d). By the chain rule:

ψ^{1/d}(x) = Σ_j A_j s_j(x)/g_j.

- **Status: correct** under H-a, H-c and H-d.

**Ratio.** By the quotient rule, with μ > 0:

ψ^T = (ψ^{1/d} − R·(v − μ))/μ.

- **Status: correct.**
- The reference part carries 0.9–4.4% of var(ψ) in the synthetic worlds. It is kept because it is mean-type and may be more persistent than the fit part.

### 2.5 Plug-in

The series is evaluated with m̂_j, θ̂ (a tight refit; §5) and ĝ_j. This is a consistent plug-in.
- acf(1) and the block-variance ratios do not depend on the scale of ψ. They depend on ĝ only through the relative weights A_j/ĝ_j.
- The SE reading depends on ĝ at first order.
- Both effects are measured in §3.2.

## 4. The case value, and how to read H1a's coverage

**The case value depends on the cross-size correlation.** Assume a Gaussian copula with a common latent lag-1 φ in every component (state factor and per-size parts). Then the sign covariances are (1/2π)·arcsin(·) of the latent correlations (Sheppard), and

ℓ(φ) = Σ_jk w_j w_k arcsin(φ ρ_jk) / Σ_jk w_j w_k arcsin(ρ_jk), with w = A/g.

- At latent φ = 0.3 and exchangeable ρ_jk = c² (`ma1_ift.log`):
  - c² = 0 (independent sizes): ℓ = (2/π)·arcsin 0.3 = **0.194**;
  - c² = 0.49: ℓ ≈ 0.15. The simulated population value is 0.1547 / 0.1502 against the formula's 0.1543 / 0.1490;
  - c² → 1: ℓ falls to **0.022** with equal g, and to 0.036–0.109 with the κ density pattern.
- The fall comes from Σ_j A_j = 0. A strongly shared state factor cancels in ψ, so latent dependence reaches ψ only through the per-size parts.

**Consequences for D1.**
1. **No tabulated coverage exists for H1a.** WU-0006 has no H1a case: a nonlinear fit of medians with a ratio. E2 lists none, so the E2 interpolation has no (cov₀, cov₀.₃) pair to use.
2. **Borrowing a case value is not admissible.** C3's ℓ_c = 0.19 is the largest case value H1a can have. With E2's rule, a larger ℓ_c gives a smaller stress fraction ℓ̂/ℓ_c, so borrowing it is the looser choice (PRIME.2).
3. **A per-cell case value is not readable at pilot n.** It could be computed (ρ̂_jk = sin(2π·mean(s_j s_k)) from the dumped signs). But when ℓ_c ≲ 0.06, the SD of ℓ̂ (0.055 at n = 300) makes ℓ̂/ℓ_c noise.
4. **What decides coverage is ψ's own dependence, not the latent φ.** ψ is exactly what the bootstrap resamples (§3.2). The latent-φ indexing of the WU-0006 rows is a calibration device, not the estimand.

**Reading for H1a** (per system and ρ, on ψ):
- acf(1) against the null SD 1/√n and the M4.2 thresholds;
- β̂₅ = [1 + 2Σ_{k=1}^{4}(1 − k/5)φ̂^k](1 − φ̂)/(1 + φ̂), with φ̂ = acf(1), and its first-order coverage 2Φ(z√β̂₅) − 1. That is 0.950 at β = 1, 0.942 at β₅ = 0.937 (the §3.2 worlds at φ = 0.3), and 0.932 at β₅ = 0.8685 (M4.2's stress), at 95%;
- the M4.2 rule "block length 5 is a design question" when β̂₅ + 2 SE < 0.8685;
- D2's variance ratios as for every series;
- the label "coverage not tabulated (no WU-0006 case for H1a); φ = 0 reading only", as for Q-N, H1b and H1c.

**What the rehearsal still cannot diagnose for H1a:**
- the coverage level itself (finite-n bias of the median fit, up to +0.018 = 0.1 SD at n = 300 in §3.2; percentile-interval asymmetry). That would need a WU-0006-style case, which is a planner and owner question and is not proposed here;
- dependence in the common state difficulty. By §2.3 it does not reach H1a's interval, so this is not a gap for H1a;
- cells with the exponent at a bound, or with H not positive definite: there ψ is "not computable" (H-c, H-d).

## 5. Recommendation for .8 (`tools/rehearsal_diagnostics.py`)

**Procedure, per system and ρ.** It replaces "H1a not computable" in `OUTSIDE_SCHEMA`.

1. **Domain.** keep = rows where every `h1a/rho=*/seed_sq` and `h1a/inverse_dim` is finite (the production filter, one set for all ρ). The series runs over keep in time order, **compacted, not zero-filled**, because the production bootstrap resamples the compacted array. Print `n_excluded`.
   - The M4.1 "0 outside the domain" form is for criteria whose bootstrap runs over the whole split. For H1a the two forms coincide when n_excluded = 0.
2. **Reproduce the leaves.** m̂_j = `np.median(seed_sq[keep], 0)`; μ̂ = mean of `inverse_dim[keep]`. Reproduce `median_seed_by_n_lib`, `inverse_dim_reference` and `fitted_inverse_dim` through the production fit, as .7c b3 does.
3. **Point fit at a bound** (`n_exponent_at_bound.point` = 1): "not computable (exponent at a bound)".
4. **Tight refit.** TRF with the analytic Jacobian, ftol = xtol = gtol = 1e-15, from the production guess, then Newton steps with the exact Hessian (`ma1_core.fit_tight`). Require |b*/2 − `fitted_inverse_dim`| ≤ 4e-5·max(|lo|, |hi|) of `ci_inverse_dim` (the Q1 a-priori bound; the pilots show ≤ 2.3e-6·s). Otherwise "not computable (refit differs)", with the difference printed.
5. **Fit weights.** Form H as in §2.3. Require λ_min > 0 and cond(H) ≤ 1e8; otherwise "not computable". Then A = ½·(H⁻¹Jᵀ)[1].
   - **Do not** use the Gauss–Newton form (24% error).
   - **Do not** take finite differences of the production fit (Q1 noise, 3.7e-3).
6. **Densities.** ĝ_j by SBG on log e²: h = 1.96^{2/3}·(1.5/(2π))^{1/3}·n^{−1/3} = 0.97157·n^{−1/3}, and 1/ĝ = (Q_{½+h} − Q_{½−h})/(2h), with numpy's default quantile. A zero spread gives "not computable".
7. **Series.** ψ by the §0 formula, with R̂ the `ratio` leaf.
8. **Readings.** The §4 readings. Print beside them:
   - SE_iid and SE_MBB5 of ψ against (hi − lo)/3.92 of `ratio_ci`, as information;
   - the series count. H1a adds 6 series (3 systems × 2 ρ), so M4.2's familywise count becomes 24 series at the pilot.
