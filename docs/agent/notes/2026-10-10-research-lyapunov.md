## Units and conversion to the code's convention

- Every published value above is per unit of the system's natural time variable. The code's time is in steps, with Delta in natural time units per step (`SystemSpec.max_dt` and the `interval` argument, "natural time units" in the docstrings).
- The code sets Delta = 1/(30 * lambda_max), so one Lyapunov time is 30 steps and lambda_max per step equals 1/30 = 0.0333 by construction. The conversion is lambda_step = lambda_time * Delta, and the number of steps per Lyapunov time is 1/lambda_step. Any error in lambda_max moves Delta in proportion (a 1% error in lambda_max is a 1% error in Delta, and the actual Lyapunov time is then 30 * lambda_code / lambda_true steps).
- Delta that follows from each value (natural time units), for orientation only: Lorenz 0.9056 gives 0.0368; Rössler 0.0714 gives 0.4669, 0.0706 gives 0.4721, 0.0719 gives 0.4636; Chua 0.431 gives 0.0773.
- Because the code computes lambda_max itself, the validation in WU-0007.2 compares the code's estimate with the published value in natural time units. It does not compare in steps.

### What "our system" is (read from `lpcstudy/systems.py` lines 74-101 and 145-148 and Methods v2 §M2 lines 69-73)

No gamma term (gamma = 0). This is the Kennedy/Chua dimensionless form. Parameters: alpha=15.6, beta=28.0, m0=-1.143, m1=-0.714. ### Mismatch analysis for candidate 2 (the only primary exponent found)

Derived by me, not stated in the memo: the memo's equations (1.1) with C1 = 1/9, C2 = 1, L = 1/7, G = 0.7 map to Chua's dimensionless form by tau = t G / C2 = 0.7 t, alpha = C2/C1 = 9, beta = C2/(L G^2) = 7/0.49 = 14.286 (= 100/7), and the diode slopes divided by G. From memo p.2, (1.2), g(v) = m1 v inside |v| < B_p (I checked: for |v| < B_p, g = m0 v + (m1-m0)v = m1 v) and m0 outside, so the memo's inner slope is -0.8 and outer slope is -0.5. Divided by G = 0.7 these are -8/7 inner and -5/7 outer, which are our code's m0 (inner) = -1.143 and m1 (outer) = -0.714 in our naming. Note that the memo's m0/m1 labels are swapped relative to our code's (the memo calls the outer slope m0; the code calls the inner slope m0).

So the memo's set equals alpha = 9, beta = 100/7, m0 = -8/7, m1 = -5/7: the m values agree with ours, alpha and beta do not (15.6 and 28 in the code). If the memo's mu_1 = 0.23 is per unit of memo time, then per unit of dimensionless time it is 0.23 / 0.7 = 0.33 (derived, unconfirmed; two digits, with the memo's own caveat that convergence at T = 3000 was still slow and the sum was -0.05, not clearly the -divergence). That 0.33 is not our system and cannot validate lambda_max at alpha = 15.6, beta = 28. It does give a published case (alpha = 9, beta = 100/7, m0 = -8/7, m1 = -5/7) on which the code's Lyapunov routine could be checked, if the owner wants a primary reference for the Chua field and the Benettin implementation. The comparison would need the memo's time convention (divide by 0.7) and a tolerance that reflects two digits and slow convergence. This is a suggestion for the caller; it is not tested.

### Dimensionless time convention

- The code's Chua time is the standard dimensionless time (tau = t G / C2) in which the equations take the alpha, beta form with unit coefficient on the y - x and x - y + z terms. A published exponent is comparable only if it is per unit of that same tau. The memo's time is a scaled circuit time (factor 0.7 above), so memo exponents are not directly per unit tau.
- Candidates 8 and 9 do not state their time unit for the exponent (they state dt = 0.05 and 0.02, which suggests the dimensionless tau, but I did not verify that).

### Task 1: what the memo says (verbatim, read directly)

Citation: T. Matsumoto, L. O. Chua and M. Komuro, "A Double-Scroll Chaotic Attractor from a Third Order Reciprocal Circuit", Memorandum No. UCB/ERL M84/73, 31 August 1984, Electronics Research Laboratory, University of California, Berkeley. The title page is `A DOUBLE-SCROLL CHAOTIC ATTRACTOR FROM A THIRD ORDER RECIPROCAL CIRCUIT`, not "The Double Scroll"; the IEEE paper "The Double Scroll" (CAS-32(8) 1985) is a different document and was not read.

- Equations, p.2, boxed (1.1): "C1 dv_C1/dt = G(v_C2 - v_C1) - g(v_C1);  C2 dv_C2/dt = G(v_C1 - v_C2) + i_L;  L di_L/dt = -v_C2". Text: "where v_C1, v_C2 and i_L denote voltage across C1, voltage across C2 and current through L, respectively and g(v_C1) is the piecewise-linear function in Fig. 1(b)".
- Nonlinearity, p.2, (1.2): "g(v_C1) = m0 v_C1 + (1/2)(m1 - m0)|v_C1 + B_p| + (1/2)(m0 - m1)|v_C1 - B_p|".
- Parameters, p.2, (1.3): "Figure 2 shows the chaotic attractor observed by solving (1.1) with 1/C1 = 9, 1/C2 = 1, 1/L = 7, G = 0.7, m0 = -0.5, m1 = -0.8, B_p = 1." (The comma and line breaks are as in the scan.)
- Slopes (my algebra from (1.2), checked): for |v| < B_p, g = m1 v, so the inner slope is m1 = -0.8; for |v| > B_p, g = m0 v + (m1 - m0) B_p sign(v), so the outer slope is m0 = -0.5. Equilibria at v_C1 = 0 and +-1.5 (from -G v = g(v): -0.7 v = -0.5 v - 0.3). This matches the equilibria (+-1.5, 0, -+1.5) that Martins finds for the same set.
- Persistence, p.4: ranges of 1/C1, 1/C2, 1/L and G over which the attractor persists around (1.3). A second set (1.4), p.5: "1/C1=10, 1/C2=0.5, 1/L=7, G=0.7, m0=-0.1, m1=-4, B_p=1". p.5: "Let us explain why we chose (1.3) instead of (1.4)".
- Equilibrium eigenvalues, p.6: "With (1.3), the eigen values are gamma_p = -2.76; alpha_p +- j beta_p = 0.13 +- j 2.13; gamma_o = 1.55; alpha_o +- j beta_o = -0.68 +- j 1.90". - Jacobian, Appendix p.27: rows "[-(1/C1)(G + (Dg)_vC1), (1/C1)G, 0]; [(1/C2)G, -(1/C2)G, 1/C2]; [0, -1/L, 0]". Its trace is -(G/C1 + G/C2) - (Dg)/C1.
- Method, pp.19-24: variational equation (3.12), renormalisation (3.16)-(3.17). p.23: "In our case, we chose tau = 10 with the Runge-Kutta step size 0.005. Our experience indicates that (3.14) is insensitive to the initial tangent vector e and to the initial condition x0. The time T = 3000 seems to be enough for the convergence." (tau = 10 is the renormalisation interval in the memo's time units.) p.22, (3.14)-(3.15): "mu_1(x_0) ~ 0.23 where x_0 = (-1.7713, 0.0527854, 1.74606), e = (1/sqrt3, 1/sqrt3, 1/sqrt3), T = 3000". p.23, (3.19): "mu_1(x_0) + mu_2(x_0) ~ 0.23". p.24, (3.20)-(3.22): "Finally, observing that (D phi^t) ^ (D phi^t) ^ (D phi^t) = det(D phi^t) and d/dt det(D phi^t) = trace(DF) det(D phi^t) one can compute (3.11). Our computation with the same x_0 and T gives mu_1 + mu_2 + mu_3 ~ -0.05 (3.22). The convergence, however, seems to be very slow. At T = 3000 it is still increasing in an extremely slow manner. Further computations would not be very meaningful for numerical reasons. It is enough for our present purpose. It follows from (3.14), (3.19) and (3.22) that mu_1 ~ 0.23, mu_2 ~ 0, mu_3 ~ -0.28 (3.23)". Then: "This shows that in the attractor observed, certain line elements are expanded, area elements are preserved and volume elements are contracted."

### Task 2: the form, the time unit, and the consistency check

- Form: dimensional circuit equations (1.1) with C1 = 1/9, C2 = 1, L = 1/7, G = 0.7 (so conductance and capacitance are of order 1 and no rescaling is applied by the authors). Time t is the circuit time of (1.1). The exponents mu_k are per unit of this t (natural log, p.18 footnote). The step 0.005 and renormalisation interval 10 are in this same t.
- Mapping to the dimensionless form (derived; I checked the algebra): tau = G t / C2 = 0.7 t, alpha = C2/C1 = 9, beta = C2/(L G^2) = 7/0.49 = 100/7, inner slope -0.8/0.7 = -8/7, outer slope -0.5/0.7 = -5/7. Exponents per unit tau equal exponents per unit t divided by 0.7. So mu_1 = 0.23 per t is 0.33 per tau (0.321 to 0.336 for the rounding range 0.225 to 0.235). Earlier note: this part is right.
- What mu_1, mu_2, mu_3 are: Lyapunov exponents of the flow (1.1) in decreasing order, with mu_1 the largest, mu_2 the zero exponent along the flow, mu_3 the most negative. d_L is the Kaplan-Yorke (Lyapunov) dimension with the formula quoted at p.25.
- Arithmetic of the memo's own numbers: (0.23 + 0)/0.28 = 0.821, so d_L = 2.82. So (3.25) is consistent with (3.23). Likewise mu_3 = -0.05 - 0.23 - 0 = -0.28 is how (3.23) was obtained, so (3.23) is consistent with (3.19) and (3.22). The inconsistency is in (3.22) itself, not in the arithmetic chain.
- The inconsistency. The sum of the three exponents of a flow equals the long-time average of trace(DF) along the trajectory (the memo states this itself in (3.20)-(3.22) and, for Lorenz, in "divergence = const"). From the memo's own Jacobian (p.27) with (1.3): trace = -(0.7)(9) - (0.7)(1) - 9 Dg = -7 - 9 Dg. In the inner region Dg = m1 = -0.8, trace = -7 + 7.2 = +0.2. In the outer regions Dg = m0 = -0.5, trace = -7 + 4.5 = -2.5. The memo's own p.6 eigenvalues confirm both values: at P: -2.76 + 2(0.13) = -2.50; at the origin: 1.55 + 2(-0.68) = +0.19. So along any trajectory the average divergence lies between -2.5 and +0.2. A sum of -0.05 then requires the trajectory to spend a fraction p of its time in the inner region with 0.2 p - 2.5 (1 - p) = -0.05, i.e. p = 0.907. The memo describes the attractor as rotating around P+ or P- (outer regions) with brief passages through D0 (p.2-3 and p.26), so p = 0.9 is not credible. The applied-mathematician's value, about -1.54 per t (the exponents computed with the code's integrator are (0.2265, 0.0006, -1.768) per t), corresponds to p = (2.5 - 1.54)/2.7 = 0.36, which is plausible. I did not compute these myself: I have no shell. They are the applied-mathematician's numbers, quoted from the task brief.
- This is not a units problem. All three quoted numbers (mu_1, the sum, and mu_3) are in the same unit (the memo's t) and the ratio of the sum to mu_1 does not depend on the unit. If the memo's exponents were per tau instead of per t, the sum would be divided by 0.7 too and still be near -0.07, not -2.2. A rescaling of all three by any common factor cannot fix it. It is also not a different parameter set within the memo (p.5): for (1.4) the divergence range would be -6.35 to +32.65, where -0.05 is possible in principle, but x_0 and the observed mu_1 = 0.23 (matches the (1.3) computation) point to (1.3). I cannot rule out (1.4) from the text alone.
- Most likely explanation (my judgement, label: unconfirmed): an error in the memo's volume-element computation (3.10)-(3.11) or (3.20)-(3.22). The memo itself flags this one, and only this one, as "very slow" and "still increasing", although a time average of a bounded piecewise-constant divergence over T = 3000 converges quickly (the error shrinks like the occupation-time fluctuation, 1/sqrt(T) at worst, not "extremely slowly"). The Lorenz sum (-21.00 = 1.37 - 22.37) is exact, so the method is right where the divergence is constant; for Chua, where the divergence is piecewise, a mis-handled trace term (for example evaluating Dg at the wrong point, or a sign) is a plausible cause. I cannot verify any of this from the memo. A typo of mu_3 (-0.28 instead of about -1.8) is also possible but is less likely, because (3.22) and (3.25) are then also affected (d_L = 2.82 follows from -0.28, and with mu_3 = -1.77 it would be 2.13). The values mu_3 = -1.77 and d_L = 2.13 are the applied-mathematician's code result and my arithmetic on it, not memo values.
- Can it be resolved from the memo alone? No. Resolution would need the IEEE paper (CAS-32(8) 797-818), which may repeat or correct the numbers (not retrieved), or an independent computation (the code's own routine, already done by the applied-mathematician).
