## Request 4. Letellier-Aguirre-Maquet observability (CONFIRMED on secondary sources)

PMC full text, Section 2.2, Example 2, fetch extract: "delta_x = 0.022, delta_y = 0.133 and delta_z = 0.006" and "the variables of the Rössler system can be ranked according to observability as y > x > z" (values attributed to Letellier et al. 2005; the same sentence reads "the difficulties of using z to observe the system"). Definition, Eq. (12): delta = |lambda_min[O^T O]| / |lambda_max[O^T O]|, a ratio of extreme eigenvalues of the observability matrix Gram product. Eq. (14) averages it along a trajectory.
- Caveat 1: the parameter set of Example 2 is not stated in the extract ("(a, b, c) are parameters"); the only explicit set in the paper, Section 4.2, is (a, b, c) = (0.398, 2, 4), where the order is "delta_y > delta_x > delta_z". So the ranking holds in both places, but the three numbers cannot be tied to the classic (0.2, 0.2, 5.7) from the retrieved text.
- Caveat 2: Section 4.2 says the coefficients "are not comparable in general among different systems"; only the ranking within a system is meaningful.
- Caveat 3: z is singular on the plane z = 0 (a statement in a search snippet, determinant of the z-observability matrix = -z^2; not verified by me).

**Lorenz.** P. Gonzalez, C. Lainscsek, T. Sejnowski, C. Letellier (arXiv:2009.11759, "Assessing observability of chaotic systems using Delay Differential Analysis"), fetch extract of Section IV: "variable x was correctly detected as providing the best observability" and "variable z was found to offer worse observability than variable y", which the paper attributes to the symmetry, "a feature which is not predicted by the SOC due to a problem inherent to the symmetry involved". It cites King & Stewart (1992) for the need to measure two variables to reconstruct a rotation symmetry. Symbolic observability coefficients there: Rössler x 0.84, y 1.0, z 0.56; Lorenz x 0.78 (y and z lower).
- Status of the mathematician's phrase "z symmetric-blind for Lorenz": supported qualitatively (z is invariant under the rotation symmetry; z-delay vectors cannot distinguish (x, y, z) from (-x, -y, z)). ## Request 7. Zhong 1994 cubic Chua (CONTRADICTED on provenance)

**What Zhong's paper gives (read directly, 10 pages).** G.-Q. Zhong, "Implementation of Chua's circuit with a cubic nonlinearity", Memorandum UCB/ERL M94/42, 28 May 1994 (http://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1994/Archive/ERL-94-42.pdf). The journal version is IEEE Trans. Circuits Syst. I 41(12), 934-941 (1994), as given in a snippet of another paper's reference list; I did not see it. It is an experimental and INSITE-simulation paper in circuit units; it contains no Lyapunov exponent. Eq. (11) p.4: g(v_c1) = a v_c1 + c v_c1^3 (a < 0, c > 0), state equations (10) with an inductor resistance R_0. Fig. 5 and 6 captions p.5-6: "C1 = 7 nF, C2 = 78 nF, L = 18.91 mH, R0 = 14.99 ohm (the internal resistance of the inductor L), a = -0.59 mS, c = 0.02 mS/V^2", with the double scroll at R = 1964 ohm. The body text p.3 gives a = -0.599 mS, c = 0.0218 mS/V^2 for the measured diode; so the memo itself has two slightly different values for a and c.

**Conversion to the dimensionless form (derived by me, arithmetic checked once).** With tau = G t / C2, G = 1/R = 1/1964 S, x = v/E (E = 1 V): alpha = C2/C1 = 78/7 = 11.14; beta = C2/(L G^2) = 15.91; gamma = R0 C2/(L G) = 0.121 (non-zero); f(x) = (a/G) x + (c E^2/G) x^3 = -1.159 x + 0.0393 x^3. These are not (alpha = 10, beta = 14.87, a = -1.27, b = 0.0157), and gamma = 0 is assumed in the candidate while Zhong's circuit has gamma about 0.12. A different voltage scale E rescales only the cubic coefficient (b = 0.0393 E^2), and cannot move a from -1.159 to -1.27, so the candidate is not a rescaling of Zhong's set.

**Where the candidate's numbers come from.** alpha = 10, beta = 14.87 and the slope -1.27 are the standard piecewise-linear Chua values: a snippet of a search result reports "a = -1.27, b = -0.68, alpha = 10.0, beta = 14.87" as the chaotic PWL set (Kahan & Sicardi-Schiffino, Physica A 1999, not opened by me), with the usual form g(x) = b x + (1/2)(a - b)(|x+1| - |x-1|). The cubic coefficient 0.0157 appears in no retrieved source. A published cubic-diode double scroll with a rigorous trapping region exists (Z. Galias and W. Tucker, ISCAS 2018, "On the existence of the double scroll attractor for the Chua's circuit with a smooth nonlinearity"; abstract via search; full text not retrievable from the Uppsala host) but I could not read its parameters.

**Status.** The statement "Zhong (1994) gives (10, 14.87, -1.27, 0.0157)" is CONTRADICTED: Zhong's set differs as above. The statement "a cubic-diode Chua is chaotic" is CONFIRMED (Zhong: double scroll, period-doubling route, simulation and experiment). A published lambda_max for any cubic set: NOT FOUND. The mathematician's own lambda of 0.277 is a development value for an unsourced parameter set; if the system is kept, cite it as "cubic approximation of the PWL double scroll, parameters chosen by the project" and not as Zhong's.

## Request 12. Levina-Bickel (PARTIAL)

E. Levina and P. J. Bickel, "Maximum likelihood estimation of intrinsic dimension", Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 17 (NIPS 2004), pp. 777-784 (MIT Press, published 2005; the note's "2005" is the proceedings year). Read directly (8 pages, https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~bickel/mldim.pdf).
- **Locally uniform Poisson hypothesis: CONFIRMED.** Section 3, p.3: "The observations represent an embedding of a lower-dimensional sample, i.e., X_i = g(Y_i), where Y_i are sampled from an unknown smooth density f on R^m, with unknown m <= p, and g is a continuous and sufficiently smooth (but not necessarily globally isometric) mapping." "The basic idea is to fix a point x, assume f(x) approx const in a small sphere S_x(R) of radius R around x, and treat the observations as a homogeneous Poisson process in S_x(R)." Estimator (8): m_k(x) = [ (1/(k-1)) sum_{j=1}^{k-1} log(T_k(x)/T_j(x)) ]^(-1), and p.4: "One could divide by k-2 rather than k-1 to make the estimator asymptotically unbiased." p.4, Section 3.1: the quantities m log(T_k/T_j) behave as exponential order statistics, so the sum is Gamma(k-1, 1) distributed (up to the factor m); this gives E[ (1/(k-1)) sum log(T_k/T_j) ] = 1/m exactly under the Poisson model, which is the "1/d" form that `oracle.inverse_dimension` uses (derived by me from this statement).

A snippet says a bias correction exists (MacKay & Ghahramani); I did not open it. ## Request 14. Continuous piecewise-linear field: C^1, not C^2 (PARTIAL)

- **Saltation formula: found.** S. A. Burden, S. Revzen, S. S. Sastry, D. E. Koditschek, "Event-selected vector field discontinuities yield piecewise-differentiable flows", arXiv:1407.1775 (SIAM J. Appl. Dyn. Syst., doi 10.1137/15M1016588; volume and pages not retrieved), ar5iv fetch extract, Section 6.1.2, Eq. (34): "X(s+) = [ I + (F_{+1}(rho) - F_{-1}(rho)) Dh(rho) / (Dh(rho) F_{-1}(rho)) ] X(s-)". That implication is my substitution into their formula, not a sentence of the paper (the extract states the paper does not say it explicitly). The paper's abstract says the flow is "Lipschitz continuous and piecewise-differentiable" for fields discontinuous across event surfaces; it also says in Section 6.1.2 that the flow "will generally not possess a classical derivative" when several surfaces are crossed at once.
- **"C^1 but not C^2": NOT FOUND.** No retrieved text states that the flow of a continuous piecewise-smooth vector field is C^1 but not C^2. The extract of Burden et al. contains no statement about second derivatives. di Bernardo, Budd, Champneys, Kowalczyk, Piecewise-smooth Dynamical Systems: Theory and Applications, Applied Mathematical Sciences 163 (Springer, 2008): existence and title confirmed in a library record; the book text was not retrieved, so the chapter and page for the saltation-identity statement and the order of the discontinuity mapping are not given. A snippet from a thesis in the UCD repository suggests higher-order discontinuity-mapping terms; not opened.
- Mathematical status of the remark in section B.4 of the note: my own reasoning (not a source) is that for a continuous, piecewise-linear field the variational equation has a coefficient matrix with a jump across |x| = 1 and the solution of the variational equation is still continuous, so the flow derivative is continuous (C^1) and its derivative jumps (not C^2); this is what the note says. It is plausible and consistent with the saltation formula above but is `unconfirmed` against a source.
