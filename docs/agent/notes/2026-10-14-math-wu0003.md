## M1. The A6 tiers

### M1.1 What can differ under each tier (from the code at e29cb16)

- **The statistics orbit** (`build_context`: 3000 + 200 000 steps from `spec.initial_state`) and the Lyapunov orbit (`lyapunov_record`: 5000 + 100 000 steps at `20·max_dt`) do not depend on the preset or the seed. They give λ, `interval`, σ_x, C, W, U, `delay`, `spacing`, and the digests `state_mean_digest`, `cov_digest`, `whiten_digest` and `unwhiten_digest`.
- **The main and estimator trajectories** start from `draw_starts(seed, system, stats, unwhiten)`, which does not depend on the preset. They run for `burn + total` and `burn + est_len` steps, which do depend on the preset. **No context digest covers them.** `trajectory_max_abs.{main, estimator}` (a float maximum) and the `start_state` leaves are the only context traces.
- **W and U** come from `np.linalg.eigh` on the host CPU on every device. They change only with the BLAS kernel (DEF-0024) or with the statistics orbit.
- **Everything jitted runs on the device:** trajectories, λ, gains, forecasts, the LM, the network training and inference, V1's AD and FD, and V2's Gauss–Newton and ν.

### M1.2 T1 (context and weights equal, device differs)

Each item, with its basis:

1. **cp and oracle chains, |Δ| ≤ 1e-12·|ref|.**
   - The device enters through g_H, the tangent propagation, at a few ulp. The analog search and whitening are host numpy, and bitwise.
   - The WU-0002.12 N1 bound (inputs at a few ulp, cancellation-amplified to ≤ 5e-13, observed 2.4e-14) applies unchanged.
   - **Confirmed**, with the scale of §M2 for leaves near 0.
2. **V1, |Δe| ≤ 1e-12 absolute: needs a premise.**
   - e = |AD − FD|/|AD| with h = 1e-6. Its round-off part is u‖f‖/(h‖f′‖) ≈ 1e-10.
   - The WU-0002.12 bound assumed the FD evaluations f(s ± h) **bitwise unchanged**, which held on one host with one XLA backend. Across devices, `delay_map_forward` and the flow are separately compiled programs. If their per-step arithmetic differs (fusion, FMA contraction), Δe ≈ u‖f‖/(h‖f′‖) ≈ 1e-10, which is 100× the tolerance, and the code is correct.
   - T1's own premise (context bitwise equal) implies the trajectory scan is bitwise equal on both devices. That makes equal f(s ± h) likely, but it is not implied: the FD calls are other programs.
   - **R0 settles it:** compare the smoke V1 leaves, GPU against CPU, for all three systems.
   - **Recommendation:** keep 1e-12. If R0 shows a V1 difference > 1e-12 at smoke, refer before R3. A bound of order 1e-9 (10× the FD round-off) would be the principled replacement, but it is wider, so it is the owner's (**OF-2**).
3. **V2, ≤ 1e-10 relative.**
   - The ν path costs κ(AᵀA)·u ≤ 2.4e-12.
   - In the numerator, the Gauss–Newton round-off is about κ(J)·u·|s| ≈ 1e-14, against ρν ≈ 1e-4. That gives ≈ 1e-10 per draw at worst, and ≈ 1e-11 after averaging 200 draws with independent rounding.
   - Observed: 2.8e-12 cross-host, 2.6e-14 cross-kernel.
   - **Confirmed** (margin 35× on the largest observation).
4. **LM per estimate, ≤ 1e-8 relative outside D1-flagged estimates: correct, but 10²× looser than the round-off bound.**
   - Two compilations of the same map differ in the residual by |Δc| ≤ η(2√c + η), with η = 1e-12·max(1, ‖X‖_∞) (WU-0005.4 M2, derived for exactly this case). At a converged estimate (c/ρ² ≈ 3.4, ρ = 0.01) that is ≈ 1e-10 relative.
   - A relative 1e-8 on c is a weak constraint on the state: since c is stationary at the minimum, δc/c = 1e-8 allows ‖JΔu‖ ≈ √(2·1e-8·c), i.e. Δŝ up to about 4e-4·ρν.
   - **Tightening (§11 item 2):** for estimates converged in both runs (not D1-flagged, c/ρ² ≤ 18.47), |Δc| ≤ η(2√c + η). The others are counted and listed with no tolerance, as planned.
   - This needs the per-estimate c in the dump, which is not listed. Add it (§11 item 8).
5. **`fraction_no_decrease` exact; `median_iterations` ±1.** Confirmed. The kernel pair shows exactly a 1.0 shift of `median_iterations` in one cell (`m1_kernel_pair.log`, V3-cp-horizon), as M7(ii) of WU-0005.4 anticipates.
6. **QoIs and interval endpoints, |Δ| ≤ 1e-4·max(|lo|, |hi|).** Evidence and amplification argument:

   | Pair | Tier type | Largest change on the primaries | Largest QoI-type change |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | Smoke Haswell vs Sandybridge (759d47b) | T2/T3 (W, U, weights differ at ulp; trajectory equal) | 5.6e-8 of max(\|lo\|, \|hi\|); 6.7e-8 of hw (H2a(ii) interior) | 4.0e-7 (H1a `ratio_ci`) |
   | DEV-0 vs v1 pilot, cross-host (WU-0008 M5) | T2/T3 plus fitted-proxy chain heads | — (primaries changed by design) | 6.0e-5 (MSE means, H2c/H2d endpoints, oracle recovery) |

   - **Amplification.** Under T1, a converged estimate differs by Δŝ ≈ κ(J)·u·|s| ≲ 1e-13 (whitened units).
     - Before saturation, a forecast error changes by Δŝ/δ: the amplifier cancels.
     - After saturation it changes by at most Δŝ·g_H/e_sat. At 8 LT, g_H ≈ e⁸ ≈ 3·10³ for a typical state. In the stretching tail (ln σ₁ over 1 LT up to 9.9, v6 note §1.2), assume up to about e¹⁵ ≈ 3·10⁶. That gives ≤ 3e-7.
     - The primaries are at 1 LT (H2b, H3c) and at 1, 2, 4 LT (H2a(i)), where the bound is ≤ 1e-9.
     - So e⁸ is harmless for the T1 QoI rule. The plan's concern applies to the 8-LT leaves without an interval (KM curves, `weight_profile`), and those have no T1 tolerance today (see §M2 (h) and §11 item 4).
   - **Where 1e-4 is thin.** The largest observed cross-host change (6e-5, margin 1.7×) sits downstream of `fitted/analog_proxy` and `fitted/estimation_proxy`. Their `least_squares` fit stops on ftol = 1e-8, so a flipped stopping test moves a coefficient by up to √ftol ≈ 1e-4 relative. The A5 tree (split order) and the K rule are the other discrete heads of H3c.
     - None of these is a recorded integer. So A6's exception ("a block where a discrete count differs") cannot see them, and a correct T2/T3 run could raise a false DEF on H2c, H2d or H3c.
     - **Recommendation (§11 item 3; no tolerance changed):** record the integer outcomes of the proxy fits (`nfev`, `status`) and a digest of the A5 leaf membership of the calibration and test states (integers) as new leaves in WU-0003.7. List every integer leaf of a result's upstream chain as a "discrete count".
     - Whether a `fitted/*` coefficient beyond 1e-6 also licenses the A4-downstream leaves, as in DEC-0019 N3 scoped causally, is the owner's decision. It widens the exception (**OF-1**).
7. **The discrete-count exception, |Δ| ≤ 0.1·hw.** Right for counts of states.
   - One state's flip moves a median by about 1/(n f(m)), which is 2/(z√n) ≈ 0.05·hw at n = 300 eligible states, and moves a mean by about 0.02–0.03·hw. So 0.1·hw allows 2 to 5 flips.
   - It is not enough for a structural flip (K, `best_single`, `chosen_width`, A5 tree), which can move H3c by O(hw). Under T1 to T3 such a flip has probability ≈ perturbation/margin ≈ 1e-8/0.05 per decision, which is negligible.
   - **Keep 0.1·hw.** A structural flip that breaks it is a DEF to root-cause and refer, as the plan says. No change.

  - H2a(ii) has two parts, so there are 18 statistics per run. At 3.4 the family level is 18 × 6.7e-4 = 1.2%.

  - At that worst inflation, the per-test false-alarm rate at 3.4 is Φ(−3.4/√1.35)·2 = 0.0034, and the family rate is about 6%. Without inflation it is 1.2%.
- **The premise.** TD compares two realizations whose estimands differ by the conditioning realization (trajectory, library noise applied to other states, networks, fitted A4/A5). Both SEs leave that out (WU-0008 sentence under the tables). The P0/P1 numbers above show it is small at pilot size. For H3c (fit-stage variability) it is the least certain, with only two pairs.

## M2. Scale floors for "relative difference" (the 10× rule)

**Why floors.** With rel = |Δ|/|ref|, a leaf near 0 turns ulp-level noise into a large ratio, and an integer-valued float jumps by 1. On the real kernel pair (`m2_floors.log`, 942 differing float leaves):

| Leaf set | Largest rel | 10× rule declares |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All float leaves, no floor | 0.111 (`median_iterations`, an integer as a float) | **1e+1** (meaningless; fires O3) |
| Point estimates without a floor (Q-N `delta_r2` relative to itself) | 1.36e-5 | 1e-3 (fires O3 spuriously) |
| **Interval class, scale max(\|lo\|, \|hi\|)** | 3.97e-7 (H1a `ratio_ci`) | **1e-5** |
| Weight-scale class, floor 0.01 | 4.99e-6 (`weight_profile` q25 at 165 steps, 5.5 LT) | 1e-4 |

**Recommended scales s, so that rel = |Δ|/s** (§11 item 4):

| Class | s | Justification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (a) Statistics with an interval: point estimate and both endpoints | max(\|lo\|, \|hi\|) of the reference interval; if lo = hi = 0, Δ = 0 is required and any other value is a discrete event | WU-0008 M5 and A6 already use it for the QoI rule; it is the natural scale of a decision against a band |
| (b) Weight-scale leaves on [0, 1] without an interval (w\* medians and quantiles, `weight_profile`, regime weights, A2 weights, `agreement`) | max(\|ref\|, 0.01) | 0.01 is below the per-state Monte Carlo SD of w\* (0.016–0.021, WU-0008 M3) and 1/5 of the V4 bar |
| (c) Fractions and probabilities (KM values, `fraction_*`, `gain_bound` fractions) | max(\|ref\|, 1/n) | One state's share |
| (d) Correlations without an interval (Spearman, Pearson, `corr_curve`) | max(\|ref\|, 1/√n) | The sampling SD of a correlation near 0 |
| (e) p-values (H3a) | max(\|ref\|, 1e-3) | One order below the decision bar 0.01 |
| (f) R²-type leaves without an interval | max(\|ref\|, 0.01) | Q-N reading resolution |
| (g) Positive scales (MSE, regret, λ, gains, horizons, slopes, non-floor fitted coefficients) | \|ref\|; an exact zero (A\* regret) requires Δ = 0 | They are bounded away from 0, or zero by construction |
| (h) Excluded from the 10× maximum, each under its own rule | — | Integer-valued leaves and counts (exact or ±1); digests and strings; V1 e (absolute 1e-12); V2 (relative 1e-10); `fitted/*/floor` and other chain-head leaves (reported); leaves in a block with a differing discrete count (0.1·hw); timing and provenance |

**How to apply the 10× rule.**
- Declare a tolerance **per class**: (a), and (b)–(g) together.
- The O3 trigger ("more than 1e-4 is a finding") applies to class (a), which is the class the A6 QoI rule governs.
- On the kernel pair (a stand-in for R1/R2) the rule gives 1e-5 for (a) and 1e-4 for (b)–(g). The latter is set by the 5.5-LT `weight_profile`, where amplification acts.
- The plan's "bitwise on the same device type" if R1 = R2 stays.

## M3. Projection factors and the C7 bands

**Factors** (`m3_projection.py`). hw_full = hw_pilot × f, with f the product of:
- **n:** √(n_pilot/n_full), with each criterion's own n.
- **B:** E hw(B = 2000)/E hw(B = 500) = 1.014 at 1 − 0.05/3 and 1.007 at 95% (`m3_bfactor.log`: the linear-quantile endpoints at B = 500 are biased inward by 1.9% and 0.9%). The noise of hw from B is 4.9% (B = 500) and 2.6% (B = 2000) at 1 − 0.05/3.
- **κ_E,** for criteria on eval-draw MSEs (H2c, H2d, H3c), with E going from 2 to 4.
  - Influence ψ_i = state part + draw part, where the draw part has variance ∝ 1/E. So κ_E² = (V_s + V_d/4)/(V_s + V_d/2), which lies in [0.5, 1], i.e. κ_E ∈ [0.707, 1].
  - **From the dump:** V_i(w) = w²E_C + (1 − w)²E_P + 2w(1 − w)C, from the oracle moments at H_ref and the per-state rule weights, is the draw-free per-state MSE. Then V_s is the long-run variance of the influence computed with V_i in place of the eval MSE, and V_d is the variance of the difference. **This needs the per-state rule weights at H_ref in the dump** (§11 item 8).
- **Q-N training size,** 900 → 3000: the WU-0006 synthetic hw ratio at fixed n_test = 1000 is 0.0155/0.0255 = 0.61 (logit) and 0.0251/0.0341 = 0.74 (indicator). It is design-dependent, so it is used as the lower end of a range whose upper end is 1.

### M4.4 Split-half on logit w\* when w\* is 0 or 1

**The problem.** logit ŵ is undefined at the clip, and the half-sample ŵ_A and ŵ_B are clipped more often than ŵ_200.

**The fix: use the unclipped optimum.** u = (E_P − C)/c from each half's moments.
- c_A = mean((e_C − e_P)²) > 0 almost surely, so u_A and u_B are defined for **every** state.
- The dump's split-half moments are exactly what is needed.

**Interior part (D4, attenuation of H2a(ii)).**
- Per state, Var(û_200) ≈ (u_A − u_B)²/4. This is unbiased to first order with 1 df, because û_200 ≈ (û_A + û_B)/2 and Var(û_A − û_B) = 4·Var(û_200).
- Delta method: v_i = (u_A − u_B)²/4/[w_i(1 − w_i)]², on the interior domain (0 < ŵ_200 < 1).
- Restrict to "delta-valid" states, w_i ± 3√((u_A − u_B)²/4) ⊂ (0, 1), and report the excluded count.
- Then A = [1 − mean(v_i)/var(ỹ)]^½, with ỹ the Q-residualized logit ŵ_200 on the domain. The bias at the band edge is 0.1·(1 − A).
- **Direction.** The excluded near-clip states are the noisiest, so this A overstates reliability: attenuation, which favours a pass, is understated. State it so.
- Report beside it the Spearman–Brown reliability 2r_AB/(1 + r_AB) on the states where u_A, u_B and u_200 are all in (0, 1), with the same caveat.

**Zero part.**
- Reliability of the indicator from the split-half covariance: r₁₀₀ = Cov(1{u_A ≤ 0}, 1{u_B ≤ 0})/Var(1{u_200 ≤ 0}).
- Spearman–Brown to 200 draws: ρ = 2r₁₀₀/(1 + r₁₀₀). Attenuation ≈ √ρ when the misclassification is nondifferential.
- It is defined for all states.

**Differential noise check.** Report Spearman(v_i, Λ̃_i | Q). A nonzero value means the noise is not classical (sign unknown, WU-0008 M3).

### M5.3 The D5 estimator (§11 item 7)

**Primary, on all test states, from the split-half moments in the dump.** It uses the production eligibility and the production median.
1. Fix the eligible set and the horizon segments from the 200-draw moments, as production does.
2. Compute the per-state range r over the eligible segment from ŵ_A (draws 1–100), ŵ_B (101–200) and ŵ_200.
3. Δ_sh = ½[med(r_A) + med(r_B)] − med(r_200). This is b(100) − b(200) at the level of the median.
4. Richardson with the conservative order p = ½: B̂(200) = Δ_sh/(2^{1/2} − 1) = 2.414·Δ_sh. Also print the order-1 value 1·Δ_sh.
   - p = ½ is the order of a zero-range state (noise-only range ∝ M^{−1/2}). p = 1 is the order of a well-separated range (bias ∝ σ²/gap).
   - p < ½ is not expected once eligibility is fixed.
5. SE by the production moving-block bootstrap over test states (blocks of 5, B = 2000), resampling states with their three ranges.

**Check, on the V4 states at 1, 2, 4 LT (M = 50, 200, 800, nested prefixes of one stream).**
- The median shifts m(M) = med(r_M) − med(r_800).
- Observed order p̂ = log₄[(m(50) − m(200))/m(200)], used for the order assumption. A p̂ outside [0.3, 1.5] means "order not determined".
- B̂_V4(200) = m(200)/(1 − 4^{−p}) with p = ½, i.e. 2·m(200).
- Also the signed mean deviation of ŵ_M − ŵ_800 at H_ref (planned in WU-0003.7).
- **Note.** "Against ŵ_800" alone understates by half, because b(800) = b(200)/2 at p = ½. The extrapolation is needed.

**Performance** (synthetic, `m5_d5sim.log`; V4-size samples only).
- The median-shift estimator at 40 states has SD 0.004–0.012, at 100 states 0.0024–0.0085, before the ×2 extrapolation.
- The mean per-state shift D̄(200) is more precise (SD 0.001–0.004) but **biased low for the median** (0.0085 against a truth of 0.0396).
- **Use the median form.**
- **The primary estimator, simulated directly** (`m5_d5split.log`, seed 20261017, same model, all eligible states).
  - At n = 320 (pilot): SD 0.0027–0.0096.
  - At n = 650–930 (full): SD 0.0015–0.0068.
  - Bias: within 0.005 of the truth when the true bias is ≈ 0, and 9–13% low when it is large (the population's order is between ½ and 1). That is conservative in neither direction, but small against its SD.

**Reading against C9** ("bias above 0.1·hw at full size means budget row 1 not below 10%"):
- "below 10%" iff |B̂(200)| + 2 SE < 0.1·hw_full;

## M7. P2(c) slack κ = 1e-13 at 4000 states

**The tolerances are per operation, deterministic and independent of n** (WU-0005.5 M4).
- A distance carries ≤ (D/2 + 2)u ≈ 6u of relative error, Δ̂ an absolute error ≤ 6u(r₁ + r₂), and the argmin can disagree with the exact one only within 12u·max(d).
- κ = 1e-13 ≈ 450u is 22× the first-order bound.
- None of these grows with the number of states or draws. The full preset keeps D = 7, core N_lib = 10⁴, ρ = 1% and M = 200, so the laws of r₁, Δ and ‖η‖ are those of the pilot, and τ_N (which depends on M and max|Y|) is unchanged.
- **The probability of a false violation is therefore the same at 4000 states as at 1400:** zero to first order, with a 22× margin.

**What does scale with n is `n_band_draws`**, a near-tie that only costs power:
- E[count] = n·M·2s·f(Δ/2), with s = κ(r₂ + ‖η‖) and the density of ‖η‖ = ρχ₇ at most 0.55/ρ.
- With (r₂ + ‖η‖)/ρ ≈ 10 (Lorenz-63: r₁/ρ ≈ 5.7, Δ/ρ ≈ 1.9, ‖η‖/ρ ≈ 2.6): P ≈ 1.1e-12 per draw.
- E[count] ≈ 8·10⁵ × 1.1e-12 ≈ 1e-6 per system at 4000 states.
- P1's `n_band_draws` and `n_ties` are 0; `tau_N_max` = 3.6e-12.

**The premise of C2** (≥ 100 switching states) scales with n: P1 Chua had 1203 of 1400.

**Recommendation:** no change. Optional, report-only: the smallest relative margin per chain item, (bound − value)/bound, as a leaf, so that a drift toward κ would show long before a violation.
