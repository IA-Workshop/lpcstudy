### Design question: acceptance test or characterization at φ = 0.3, and the same question at φ = 0
- **What the ROADMAP criterion is.** The ROADMAP row is a **method-validity** criterion: "the procedure attains nominal coverage within MC error". The reference shows, at much higher precision than R = 1000 can, that MBB-5 percentile intervals do not attain it:
  - slightly at φ = 0 (0.938–0.947 at 95%), most for the heavy-tailed log-normal cases;
  - by 1.9 to 2.7 points at φ = 0.3 for means, ratios and medians (0.923–0.931).
- **So no assertion of A1 to A3 can be read as "fail = code defect"**, at either φ. A code-verification criterion must compare the test with an independent reference of the same procedure.
- **Recommendation (the owner decides).**
  1. **Add now, before any result, a reference-consistency assertion.** This is a tightening, and it is the defect criterion. For every (case, φ, level) coverage, and every A3 rate, the observed count must lie in the "range" column above.
     - There are 64 such assertions. Each two-sided tail is 0.00025, so the family-wise false-alarm probability is at most 3.2% (Bonferroni).
     - A count outside its range is a defect candidate: DEF, class "code".
     - The ranges hold only if the harness generates data exactly as in M3 (a).
  2. **Option C, which I recommend if decided before the run** (owner amendment, QA.6, original kept). Keep A1 to A3 as **characterization rows**:
     - the ROADMAP bound is evaluated and its pass/fail is written to the result row, the Log and the WU-0008 budget, so the outcome stays visible;
     - the pytest outcome is the consistency assertion of item 1;
     - the predicted values and P(fail) above are fixed now, so every ROADMAP-bound failure is a predicted finding of class "method".

     Rationale:
     - nothing is hidden;
     - the defect test is stricter and two-sided, so it also catches an interval that is too wide;
     - the method's undercoverage goes to the budget with its size;
     - gates do not run red for a property of block length 5 that the owner has already been told about.
  3. **Option K, if the owner declines the amendment.** Keep A1 to A3 as pytest assertions at both φ, plus the consistency assertions. Each ROADMAP-bound failure opens a DEF. The DEF is classified "method (predicted)" if the count lies in the reference range, and "code" otherwise. About 12 DEFs are expected. They recur at every gate until the owner disposes of them, and an amendment after the results is the pattern PRIME.2 warns against. So if the owner wants Option C at all, it should be decided now.
- **What the owner may do about the method.** These are v3 matters, not WU-0006 ones:
  - record the effective coverages in the WU-0008 budget as a known limitation;
  - amend v3 to a studentized or BCa block bootstrap for the MSE-based criteria (H2c, H2d, H3c);
  - amend v3 to a longer block, once O4 is measured.

## M3. Synthetic designs

- **(a) Generator specification.** The reference used exactly this, and the ranges apply only to it.
  - AR(1): x₀ ~ N(0, 1) and x_t = φx_{t−1} + √(1 − φ²)ε_t. The bivariate version has ε_t with correlation c. All series in a case are mutually independent unless stated.
  - C2, C4, C5: a = exp(μ_a + σu) and b = exp(σv), where (u, v) is a bivariate AR(1) with c = 0.8 and σ = 1. μ_a is 0, ln 1.05 and ln 1.10 respectively. C4 uses mean(a)/mean(b); C5 uses mean(a)/mean(b) − 1; C2 uses mean(a − b).
  - C6: a bivariate Gaussian AR(1) with c = 2 sin(0.7π/6) = 0.716736.
  - C7: s₁, s₂ are independent AR(1); (e₁, e₂) is a bivariate AR(1) with c = 0.1, unit variances and independent of s. The target is t = 0.5s₁ − 0.3s₂² + e₁, w\* = expit(t) (all interior), and Λ̃ = 0.4s₂ + 0.2s₁s₂ + e₂. The seeds argument is (s₁, s₂).
  - C8, C9: the difference series is one Gaussian AR(1). A difference of two independent AR(1) with a common φ is again AR(1), so the two forms are the same design.
- **(b) σ = 1 and c = 0.8: probably too light-tailed.**
  - The per-state MSE is the mean of 4 squared errors (FULL; 2 in the pilot) times a state-dependent error variance. The log of a χ²₄/4 variable has SD √ψ′(2) = 0.80. The log amplification over 1 LT on the test-state grid has SD 1.46 (Rössler), 1.57 (Chua) and 1.89 (Lorenz), from the O4 proxy below.
  - So the log-SD of per-state MSE is plausibly 1.7 to 2.0, not 1.
  - Coverage at φ = 0 with heavier tails, as 95% / 98.3% (table above): σ = 1.5 gives 0.930–0.933 / 0.972–0.974; σ = 2 gives 0.920–0.923 / 0.967–0.970. c = 0.95 changes little.
  - **Recommendation.** Keep σ = 1 for the asserted cases, as planned, so as not to change the design. Add **C2h and C4h (σ = 1.5 and 2, c = 0.8, φ = 0)** as characterization rows (reported, no assertion, prediction fixed above). Have O4 measure the log-SD of per-state MSE. This is the most important finding for WU-0008: the intervals of H2c, H2d and H3c may run at an effective level of 0.92–0.93.
- **(c) φ on the latent or the observed series.** Keep φ on the latent, as planned: the truths are exact, the generator is simple, and it follows the ROADMAP literally.
  - The plan should, however, state the influence-series lag-1 per case (M2), because the stress differs by case.
  - O4 must measure the same quantity, the acf of the influence series, to place the study's data on this scale.
  - Calibrating the latent φ per case, so that each influence series has lag-1 0.3, is optional. For log-normal C2 it would need a latent φ of about 0.5.
- **(d) C7 construction: correct.** Both conditional means lie in span[1, Q(s)], so the population partial correlation is corr(e₁, e₂) = 0.1 exactly, whatever the variances. Centring Q on the full sample does not matter, because the span is invariant.
  - **Consequence for A5 and A3/A4.** With all w\* interior, the "zero" part has a constant indicator. It returns NaN in every resample: 2000 failed resamples, estimate NaN, "not computable". Hence `part_ii_verdict` is "inconclusive" in every replication.
  - So A5 must be asserted on the **interior column only**. A3 and A4 must use `part_ii["interior"]["verdict"]`, never `part_ii_verdict`.
  - It is worth asserting the zero column too, as an exact guard check: failed = 2000 and reason "not computable".
  - The same applies to C6: use the per-row verdict of `h1d_correlations`, not the combined one.
- **(e) C8: no bounded or zero-inflated variant is needed.** The H2b(ii) differences of w\* ∈ [0, 1] are bounded, with an atom at 0. The mean's percentile interval depends only on the second-order dependence and the tails, and bounded data are lighter-tailed than the Gaussian case, so C8 is the harder case.
  - An atom matters only for a **median** whose truth sits on the atom. For H2e, record `n_zero_difference` in the real runs. Zero-inflation of the per-state correlation change would make the C9 percentile interval degenerate. That case is not covered here, and I note it as a gap.
- **(f) Log-rank design: geometric h = 0.0125, H_max = 240.**
  - Censoring: (1 − h)²⁴⁰ = 0.0489, confirmed at 0.0489 in the simulation.
  - The pilot A5 KM curves have medians of 64–100 steps and 0–0.5% censored, with a hazard that increases with time. The log-rank test's null validity does not depend on the shape of the hazard, and the larger censoring exercises that code path more. The design is adequate.
  - The pilot chose K = 5, giving 4 Holm pairs. The ROADMAP's 3 regimes test 2 pairs. That is acceptable, because Holm's FWER control does not depend on K.
- **(g) ΔR² process.** I used, and propose:
  - x₁, x₂, x₃ and ξ are independent AR(1), and x₄ = 0.6x₁ + 0.8ξ.
  - L = x₁ − 0.5x₂² + 0.5x₂x₃ + e, with e an AR(1) of SD 1, independent of everything else.
  - w\* = 0 if L < q₁₅, the 15% quantile of L's marginal, which is −2.1471 from 2·10⁶ draws; otherwise w\* = expit(L). So logit w\* = L on the interior.
  - log_t = 0.5x₁ + 0.3x₃ + 0.5e′.
  - The training set is two independent paths, as in the pilot construction plus calibration; the test set is one path.

  This gives R²₃ = 0.35 (logit) and 0.27 (indicator), within the pilot's 0.25–0.49. Y ⊥ x₄ | x₁, x₂, x₃ holds for all three targets. ## M4. The ΔR² estimand; N1, N2, N3

- **What the code's interval covers.** For each target, `qn_profile_block` gives an approximate 95% percentile interval for the **conditional out-of-sample gain of the two fitted models**:

  Δ_cond(f̂₃, f̂₄) = [E(Y − f̂₃(X₁:₃))² − E(Y − f̂₄(X₁:₄))²] / Var(Y).

  - The expectation and the variance are over a new state from the stationary test distribution, restricted to the target's domain (interior states for the logit).
  - The fitted f̂₃ and f̂₄ (`qn_regressor(seed)` on the given training states) are held fixed.
  - The interval does not include the variability of the training set or of the learner, as v3 and Methods v2 E3 say.
  - It does not target the population gain Δ_pop = R²(E[Y | X₁:₄]) − R²(E[Y | X₁:₃]), which is 0 under the null.
- **Under the null, Δ_cond is random, with mean < 0.** This is the learner's cost for an irrelevant feature. Reference values (R = 400 per φ; R = 200 at full sizes):

## M6. Verdict error rates at the edge; exactness of A4

- **A3 is what v3 §7 implies.** For a one-sided band with edge b and the truth at b, "pass" means the interval lies on the predicted side, so P(pass | θ = b) = P(θ is beyond the predicted-side bound), the one-sided non-coverage, which is α/2 for an exact equal-tailed interval.

  - P(fail | θ = b) is the largest wrong-"fail" rate over the band, also α/2.
  - So the nominal values are 0.025 at 95% and 0.00833 at 1 − 0.05/3, and the bounds of M1 follow.
  - ~~C7's two-sided band (−0.1, 0.1) with the truth at 0.1 gives the same α/2: the half-width at 98.3% is about 0.106, so the far edge is never touched.~~ **Wrong (corrected 2026-10-11, WU-0006.4, DEF-0022; see the addendum).** The reasoning is reversed.
